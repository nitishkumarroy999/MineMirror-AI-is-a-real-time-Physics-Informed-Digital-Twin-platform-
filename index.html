The Real-World Problem

Mining still relies on reactive systems that fail when it matters most

⛏️

Rock Bursts & Collapses

Sudden geotechnical events remain hard to predict. Pure data-driven models fail because labelled failure data is extremely rare.

⚙️

Unplanned Downtime

Equipment failures cause 15–25% of available hours to be lost. Maintenance is still largely reactive or calendar-based.

👷

Occupational Health Risks

Dust, heat, noise and vibration accumulate silently until regulatory or medical thresholds are breached.

The Solution: MineMirror AI

A living digital twin powered by Physics-Informed Neural Networks

MineMirror AI continuously synchronises a high-fidelity virtual replica of the entire mine (geometry, geology, equipment fleet, ventilation and workforce) with live IoT, SCADA and wearable data. At its core, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) embed rock-mechanics and machine-physics equations into the predictive engine — enabling accurate forecasts even when historical failure data is scarce.

Predictive Safety

Early warning of rock bursts and structural instability with 2–12 hour lead time using physics-constrained models.

Equipment Digital Twins

Remaining Useful Life prediction for critical assets. Maintenance only when needed — cutting downtime by 25–40%.

Occupational Health Layer

Real-time exposure tracking (dust, heat, noise, vibration) with dynamic work-schedule optimisation.

Closed-Loop Control

Edge-executed safety interlocks with <200 ms latency. From recommendation to autonomous action under defined envelopes.

Process Optimisation

Continuous “what-if” scenarios for blast design, haulage and plant feed — maximising recovery while minimising energy and water.

Sustainability Dashboard

Live carbon, water and waste footprint with rapid scenario testing for lower-impact mining methods.

Physics-Informed Neural Networks

The scientific heart of MineMirror AI

Why PINNs Matter in Mining

Traditional AI models need large amounts of labelled failure data — which almost never exists for rare events like rock bursts. PINNs solve this by embedding the governing physical equations directly into the neural network’s loss function.

𝓛 = 𝓛data + λ · 𝓛PDE + 𝓛BC/IC

The network is forced to satisfy both the sensor measurements and the laws of physics.

Physically consistent forecasts
Generalises to new geology
